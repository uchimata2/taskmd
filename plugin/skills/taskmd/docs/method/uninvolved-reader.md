# the uninvolved reader

> An instrument, not a phase. Spine: [`../METHOD.md`](../METHOD.md).
> It is one way of meeting [`implement`](implement.md)'s exit criterion for a document, and it
> changes no rule there.

Some outcomes are documents, and a document cannot be verified by its author. Reading your own work
cannot surprise you: you supply from memory every fact the page leaves out, and the gaps that will
stop somebody else are the ones you cannot see. **So hand it to someone who was not involved, ask
them to use it, and record what happened.**

This is what [`implement`](implement.md)'s table means by *a reader who was not involved states the
conclusion back*. That row says the instrument exists; this file says how to run it without
producing a result you were always going to get.

## When to reach for it

When the deliverable is a document somebody else has to **act on** — a procedure, a contract, a
specification, a set of instructions — and being wrong about whether it is followable is expensive.

Not for prose whose only test is taste. This measures whether a document can be used, and it has
nothing to say about whether it reads well.

---

## The procedure

Each rule below was added after a run that needed it. **The runs are named by what went wrong, not
by identifier**, so that this document is readable by a project that does not have ours.

1. **Extract the document whole, and verbatim, to somewhere the reader can open without your
   project.** Not a slice, not a summary, not a link into your repository.

   *Added after a run that handed over an excerpt.* A slice tests a document nobody receives: the
   reader's questions were about context the surrounding sections supplied, so the verdict described
   a document that does not exist. Regenerate the extract each time from the live source — a copy
   kept beside the task is a second home for the document, and it is wrong the first time the
   original is edited.

2. **Give them the document and nothing else. Ask them to open nothing else.**

   Any other input — your summary, the task, a previous verdict — is you telling them what to
   conclude, slowly.

3. **Ask them to state their prior exposure in their own words**, rather than assuming it.

   *Added after a run where it was assumed.* "Uninvolved" is a claim about a person, and the person
   is the only one who can make it. It costs one sentence and it is the first thing a sceptical
   reader of the verdict will want.

4. **Fix the pass bar and the reader count before any result exists**, and write both down where
   they are dated.

   *This is the rule the whole instrument rests on.* A bar chosen after a result is a description of
   the result. Deciding it first is also what makes a **failing** verdict usable: it can be reported
   as a fail rather than argued down, because nobody could have tuned the bar to avoid it.

5. **Do not prompt for the answer you expect** — including the modest ones.

   *Added after a run whose most valuable output was a refusal.* The reader declined to name
   something they could not source, unprompted, and that refusal was worth more than either finding.
   The next run was therefore built **not** to invite one: a prompt that offers a blank as acceptable
   manufactures the blank, and then it is evidence of nothing.

6. **Record the verdict as given, including the parts that are wrong.**

   *Added after a run that returned a defect of fact rather than of framing.* A reader who
   misunderstands something has told you the document permits that misunderstanding, which is a
   result. Correct the fact in your own record, beside the quotation; never in theirs.

7. **Repair nothing inside the task that measured.**

   A document repaired while it is being read has been tested against nothing, and the record can no
   longer say which version the verdict was about. Raise the repair as its own work.
   This is [`../METHOD.md`](../METHOD.md) rule 4 arriving in a different instrument, not a second
   statement of it.

## How many readers, and the distinction the count is about

**Two readers, in parallel, on one text, with the count fixed before either runs.**

Parallel means: the same extract, at the same time, neither shown the other's answer, and no edit and
no verdict between them.

**What the count exists to prevent is a reader added *after* a result you did not like.** That is
iteration wearing an instrument's clothes — the loop is *read, dislike, read again until somebody
agrees* — and it needs an edit or a verdict in between to happen at all. Parallel running forbids it
by construction, which is why two is safe here and a second reader tomorrow is not.

**Why two rather than one.** *Corrected after a run that used two where its own rule said one, and
recorded the deviation rather than the improvement.* The two readers produced declarations that were
both shippable and **claimed different things** from the same text. That is a finding a single reader
cannot produce at any level of care: one reading tells you what one competent person concluded, and
two tell you whether the document has one meaning. The rule had been written to forbid a hazard it
described correctly and had banned the wrong thing.

**A disagreement between the two is a finding, not a tie to be broken.** Do not add a third to settle
it. Record both readings and treat the divergence as what the document did.

---

## What this cannot tell you

- **Whether the document is good.** It measures whether it can be used.
- **Whether a reader is representative.** One or two people are not a sample, and a verdict is
  evidence rather than proof. Say which you have.
- **Anything about a reader you had to brief.** If they needed context to start, the run measured a
  briefing.
- **That a repaired document is now right.** Re-running the same instrument on the repair is the
  loop rule 4 exists for — unless the count and the bar are fixed again first, and the question asked
  is the one that failed.

## Worked example — a safety briefing before a site visit

The deliverable is a two-page briefing that contractors read before arriving. The team that wrote it
cannot tell whether it is followable, because they know the site.

> **Bar, fixed first:** a reader can state where to go, what to bring, and what to do if the gate is
> locked — and names anything they had to guess. **Count, fixed first:** two, in parallel.
>
> Both were sent the briefing whole, with the question and nothing else. Both stated they had never
> visited the site.
>
> **Reader A** answered all three and guessed once, on whether the gate code was the same out of
> hours. **Reader B** answered two and could not say what to do if the gate was locked, having read
> the same sentence as covering the car park only.
>
> They disagree about what one sentence covers, which is the finding. It is recorded as a
> disagreement rather than resolved by asking a third person, and the repair is raised as its own
> work rather than made while the readings are being written up.

Note what the bar bought: Reader B's answer is a **fail** against a standard nobody could have
adjusted afterwards. And note that the guess in Reader A's answer is a finding too — the document
permitted it.
